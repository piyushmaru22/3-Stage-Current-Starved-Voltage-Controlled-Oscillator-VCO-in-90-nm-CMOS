This repository presents the comprehensive design, theoretical derivation, and simulation-based validation of a 3-stage Current-Starved Voltage-Controlled Ring Oscillator (VCO) implemented in a standard 90nm Analog CMOS technology using Cadence Virtuoso.
Unlike conventional ring oscillators whose oscillation frequency is rigidly fixed by intrinsic propagation delays, this current-starved architecture introduces tunable current-sinking and current-sourcing transistors in series with each inverter delay stage. By mirroring an external control voltage V_ctrl through a global current-mirror bias network, the circuit precisely modulates the charge and discharge currents of the load capacitance at each node. This decoupling enables an exceptionally wide, continuous frequency tuning range from 3.79 GHz to 9.75 GHz across a control voltage sweep of 0.5V to 1.2V.
A major focus of this project is the strict correlation between first-principles circuit theory and Electronic Design Automation (EDA) simulation. The design methodology rigorously derives the Barkhausen oscillation criteria for a 3-stage loop, establishes the minimum required small-signal DC loop gain A_0\2, and formulates the mathematical models governing large-signal propagation delay. These theoretical equations are directly validated against transient and parametric sweep simulations extracted from the Virtuoso Analog Design Environment.
Operating under a 1.2V supply, the final VCO architecture demonstrates robust monotonic tuning with a high average sensitivity gain K_VCO of 8.51 GHz/V. Peaking at a maximum power dissipation of just 1.26 mW, this design serves as a highly viable core topology for ultra-high-speed clock generation and GHz-range Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) integration in deep-submicron mixed-signal systems.
